\section*{Conclusion}

In this thesis, we formalized several foundational aspects of formal
group laws in Lean.  The main contribution is the development of a
Mathlib-compatible framework for formal group laws, their associated
algebraic structures, and the functional equation lemma.

We first formalized the definition of a formal group law over a
commutative ring.  In this formalization, a formal group law is represented
as a bivariate multivariate power series satisfying the usual linear
coefficient conditions and the associativity identity.  We also introduced
a typeclass formulation of commutativity, namely
\lstinline|FormalGroup.IsComm|, so that commutativity can be used as an
additional assumption on an existing formal group law rather than by
introducing a separate structure.

We then developed several algebraic structures associated to a formal
group law.  For a formal group law \lstinline|F|, we defined the type
\lstinline|F.Point σ| of points with values in multivariate power series.
The addition on these points is defined by evaluating the formal group law
\lstinline|F|.  We proved that this operation gives rise to the expected
algebraic structures, including instances of
\lstinline|AddGroup| and, under the assumption \lstinline|F.IsComm|,
\lstinline|AddCommGroup|.  This shows that the formal group law naturally
induces a group structure on suitable power series.

We also formalized homomorphisms and isomorphisms of formal group laws.
A homomorphism between two formal group laws is represented by a
power series satisfying the compatibility condition with the
two formal group laws.  An isomorphism is then packaged as a homomorphism
together with an inverse homomorphism and proofs that the two compositions
are identities.  These definitions provide the basic categorical language
needed to compare formal group laws.

Another central part of the project is the formalization of the
functional equation lemma.  We defined the power series \lstinline|RecurFun|
by specifying its coefficients recursively, and then proved that it
satisfies the desired functional equation.  We also formalized the descent
statement showing that the power series
\[
  F_g(X,Y) = f_g^{-1}(f_g(X) + f_g(Y))
\]
has coefficients in the required subring.  This completes the
formalization of the functional equation lemma in Lean.

In addition, this thesis develops several supporting results about
multivariate power series.  These include results about the order of
substitution, the expansion operation, the tactic
\lstinline|name_power_vars| for naming power series variables,
substitution inverses and so on.  These auxiliary results are not only useful for
the formalization of formal group laws, but also provide reusable
infrastructure for future work involving power series in mathlib.

Finally, we introduced the definition and basic properties of the height
of a formal group law.  This provides a bridge from the basic algebraic
theory of formal group laws to deeper classification results and
arithmetic applications.

\section*{Future Work}

There are several natural directions for future work.

One important direction is the application of formal group laws to
Lubin--Tate theory.  Lubin--Tate theory constructs highly structured
extensions of local fields using formal group laws.  A formalization of
this theory would require combining the formal group law API developed
here with mathlib's existing theory of local fields, valuations, and
ramification.  This would be a significant step toward the formalization
of local class field theory.

Another direction is the study of formal group laws arising from elliptic
curves.  Every elliptic curve has an associated formal group law obtained
by completing the curve at the identity point.  In positive characteristic,
the height of this formal group law reflects important arithmetic
information about the elliptic curve.  For example, over a field of
positive characteristic, the formal group law of an elliptic curve has
height $1$ in the ordinary case and height $2$ in the supersingular case.
Formalizing this connection would provide a useful link between the theory
of formal group laws and the existing formalization of elliptic curves.

Another further goal is the formalization of Lazard's theorem.  Lazard's
theorem states that there is a universal formal group law and identifies
its coefficient ring.  This theorem is fundamental in the algebraic theory
of formal group laws and plays an important role in algebraic topology,
especially in complex cobordism.  Formalizing Lazard's theorem would
require a substantial development of universal constructions for formal
group laws and a careful treatment of the Lazard ring.

Another possible direction is the classification of formal group laws by
height.  Over suitable fields of positive characteristic, the height is a
central invariant of a formal group law.  A natural long-term goal is to
formalize classification theorems for formal group laws of the same
height, including the construction of isomorphisms after suitable base
change.  This would extend the basic theory developed in this thesis to a
more structural understanding of formal group laws.

More generally, the results of this thesis provide a foundation for
further formalization of the arithmetic and geometric applications of
formal group laws.  The definitions of formal group laws, their points,
homomorphisms, isomorphisms, height, and the functional equation lemma can
serve as reusable infrastructure for future developments in algebraic
number theory, algebraic geometry, and arithmetic geometry.
