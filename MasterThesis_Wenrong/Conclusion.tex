\section*{Conclusion}

In this thesis, we developed a Mathlib-compatible framework for formal
group laws in Lean.  Our main results can be summarized as follows.

First, we formalized formal group laws over a commutative ring together
with their commutativity, homomorphisms, and isomorphisms, providing a
foundational API for working with formal group laws in Lean.

Second, we proved that a formal group law induces a group structure on
its points, which is commutative whenever the law is.  This shows that
the algebraic structure expected of formal group laws can be recovered
and used formally.

Third, we formalized the functional equation lemma, including both the
construction satisfying the functional equation and the descent
statement that the resulting formal group law has coefficients in the
required subring.

Along the way, we established a number of supporting results about
multivariate power series, which provide reusable infrastructure for
future work involving power series in Mathlib.  Finally, we formalized
the height of a formal group law, bridging the basic algebraic theory to
deeper classification results and arithmetic applications.

\section*{Future Work}

There are several natural directions for future work.

One important direction is the application of formal group laws to
Lubin--Tate theory.  Lubin--Tate theory constructs highly structured
extensions of local fields using formal group laws.  A formalization of
this theory would require combining the formal group law API developed
here with Mathlib's existing theory of local fields, valuations, and
ramification.  This would be a significant step toward the formalization
of explicit local class field theory.

Another direction is the study of formal group laws arising from elliptic
curves.  Every elliptic curve has an associated formal group law obtained
by completing the curve at the identity point.  In positive characteristic,
the height of this formal group law reflects important arithmetic
information about the elliptic curve.  For example, over a field of
positive characteristic, the formal group law of an elliptic curve has
height $1$ in the ordinary case and height $2$ in the supersingular case.
Formalizing this connection would provide a useful link between the theory
of formal group laws and the existing formalization of elliptic curves.

A further goal is the formalization of Lazard's theorem.  Lazard's
theorem states that there is a universal formal group law and identifies
its coefficient ring.  This theorem is fundamental in the algebraic theory
of formal group laws and plays an important role in algebraic topology,
especially in complex cobordism.  Formalizing Lazard's theorem would
require a substantial development of universal constructions for formal
group laws and a careful treatment of the Lazard ring.

A fourth direction is the classification of formal group laws by
height.  Over suitable fields of positive characteristic, the height is a
central invariant of a formal group law.  A natural long-term goal is to
formalize classification theorems for formal group laws of the same
height, including the construction of isomorphisms after suitable base
change.  This would extend the basic theory developed in this thesis to a
more structural understanding of formal group laws.

More generally, the results of this thesis provide a foundation for
further formalization of the arithmetic and geometric applications of
formal group laws.  The definitions of formal group laws, their points,
homomorphisms, isomorphisms, height, and the functional equation lemma can
serve as reusable infrastructure for future developments in algebraic
number theory, algebraic geometry, and arithmetic geometry.
